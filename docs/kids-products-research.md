# Kids-product campaign research

This set should feel like three different corners of a child's media world in
1998-2000, not three reskins of one "kids" template:

- **Rocketbox Toys (1998):** the physical toy aisle and holiday catalog.
- **Moon Munch Cereal (1999):** the cereal box as a portal to prizes and games.
- **ToonBurst Network (2000):** the television schedule turned into an online
  clubhouse.

The period evidence supports that split. Mattel's 1998 Barbie expansion added
creative activities, voting, chat, and even downloadable phrases for a physical
toy. General Mills ran large on-box game promotions in 1999, while contemporary
food promotions combined voting, sweepstakes, and online activities. Fox Kids'
1998 relaunch used Flash 3 to make a themed "Cyber-City" containing games,
downloads, behind-the-scenes material, previews, schedules, and contests.

## Shared era rules

- Design for a fixed 640-760 pixel stage, with compressed image assets and
  strong visual boundaries instead of modern full-width sections.
- Navigation should be made from branded objects: blister-card tabs, cereal
  planets, or TV control buttons. Do not reuse one navigation bar.
- Lead with a slogan and an activity, not a corporate mission statement.
- Use short, exclamatory copy: `NEW!`, `COLLECT THEM ALL!`, `CLICK TO ENTER`,
  `SATURDAY!`, `FREE INSIDE!`.
- Include one slightly impractical promotional feature per site. Authentic
  children's sites often existed to keep a campaign going rather than to make
  product information efficient.
- Keep the owner comment thread only on the domain homepage. The subpage should
  be product/schedule content, not a separate social thread.
- Make one art sheet per company. The three companies need different source
  media and should not share a single illustration style.

## Rocketbox Toys - modular action-toy system

### Market position

Rocketbox is the 1998 action-figure line that sells *combinations*, not only
characters. Figures, armor, wheels, wings, cockpit shells, and spring-loaded
tools use the same "R-Lock" connection points. It competes on the fantasy that a
child can rebuild the whole line into something the package never pictured.

Core audience: ages 7-12, with copy aimed at kids but enough diagrams and
inventory detail for a parent or collector.

Tagline: **BUILD IT. BREAK IT. BUILD IT BIGGER.**

Secondary lines:

- `Every R-Lock piece fits every Rocketbox rig.`
- `Six heroes. Six machines. 4,096 impossible combinations.`
- `You don't need instructions. You need more parts.`

### Visual language

- Source medium: flash-lit toy-catalog and blister-package photography, with
  small painted explosion/laser effects added over the photos.
- Palette: safety yellow `#ffd21c`, saturated red `#ed2724`, cobalt
  `#1746a2`, black, and off-white catalog paper.
- Typography: heavy condensed capitals for headlines, Arial/Verdana for product
  facts, tiny condensed SKU/age labels.
- Layout: dense catalog grid, diagonal yellow-and-black stripes, starburst price
  stickers, plastic-card tabs, numbered callout arrows, and a small legal footer.
- Product photos should look tangible: visible joints, molded seams, stickers,
  screws, translucent plastic, and slightly imperfect catalog shadows.
- Avoid pixel art and polished modern 3D rendering. The page should look built
  from scanned package art and 35mm catalog transparencies.

### Site structure

Suggested URLs:

- `web://rocketbox.toys/home` - launch campaign, flagship combination, owner
  thread, links into the product line.
- `web://rocketbox.toys/rigs` - catalog/combinator subpage with figures,
  vehicles, compatibility chart, and "secret build."

Homepage modules:

1. Huge photographed **MEGA RIG 01: STARBREAKER** hero with numbered parts.
2. `WHAT IS R-LOCK?` three-step diagram: snap, twist, launch.
3. Scrolling/new-product strip.
4. "RIG OF THE MONTH" reader build with an obviously mailed-in photo.
5. Tiny parent link and age/small-parts warning.

### Fictional product line

- **Nova Jack** - red flight leader; includes flare cannon and visor.
- **Circuit** - blue engineer; includes repair arm and transparent tool pack.
- **Grav-Rex** - black/purple heavy unit; includes clamp fists.
- **Solar Scout** - yellow reconnaissance figure; includes glider fins.
- **Starbreaker Rig** - flagship six-wheel rover that converts to a jet.
- **Orbit Claw** - lower-price cockpit and mechanical arm set.
- **R-Lock Booster Pack A** - wings, wheels, missiles, and connectors.

Use believable 1998 prices: figures `$5.99`, small rigs `$12.99`, Starbreaker
`$29.99`, booster packs `$7.99`. Add `Ages 5+` and a mail-in offer for a chrome
connector after collecting three UPC symbols.

### Persona direction

Suggested owner: **`Rivet_Rocketbox`**, a product-demo host/webmaster who writes
like an excited toy-fair demonstrator.

- Knows compatibility, package contents, prices, and rumored upcoming colors.
- Loves strange player-proposed combinations and names them on the spot.
- Gives clear safety answers without dropping the energetic voice.
- Is defensive about parts popping loose and insists this is a feature of the
  rebuild system.
- Short-response flavor: capitals for one or two words, mechanical verbs, and
  occasional `K-CHUNK!`; never baby talk.

## Moon Munch Cereal - prize-driven space breakfast

### Market position

Moon Munch is a loud 1999 sugar cereal whose real product is the campaign
ecosystem: mascot, collectible marshmallow shapes, box-back puzzles, prizes, and
a rudimentary web game. It is the most commercial and hyperactive of the three
sites.

Core audience: ages 6-11. Parents are addressed only in nutrition and rules
copy. Kids are addressed as the mascot's mission crew.

Tagline: **CRUNCH THE WHOLE GALAXY!**

Secondary lines:

- `New comet-crunch clusters and five cosmic marshmallow shapes!`
- `FREE MISSION PATCH INSIDE! Which one did YOU get?`
- `Fuel up. Log on. Save breakfast.`

### Visual language

- Source medium: airbrushed cereal-package painting plus photographed cereal,
  milk splashes, cardboard prize callouts, and chrome foil bursts.
- Palette: midnight purple `#28115b`, electric blue `#1678d4`, radioactive lime
  `#b8ef24`, cereal orange `#f28a19`, marshmallow pink `#ff6cad`.
- Typography: inflated, irregular display lettering; Verdana body; pixel bitmap
  labels inside the web-game panel.
- Layout: a planetary map whose clickable worlds are page modules, with a
  permanently visible cereal box, mascot speech bubble, animated stars, and
  beveled `PLAY!` buttons.
- It should be cluttered and promotional, but the game and prize should remain
  the two obvious targets.
- Art sheet: painted mascot poses, package front, bowl glamour shot, individual
  marshmallow shapes, mission-patch prizes, UFO, moon surface, and button art.
  Keep this airbrush/package-painting style separate from Rocketbox photography.

### Site structure

Suggested URLs:

- `web://moonmunch.com/home` - cereal campaign, prize callout, owner thread,
  mascot introduction.
- `web://moonmunch.com/mission` - simple score game or simulated game screen,
  prize checklist, box code field, and downloadable certificate.

Homepage modules:

1. Captain Crater bursts through the cereal-box frame.
2. Bowl/photo hero with labeled shapes: rocket, moon, alien, star, and comet.
3. `WHAT'S IN YOUR BOX?` rotating mission-patch prize display.
4. `PLAY MOON MUNCH MISSION` game portal.
5. Nutrition panel and sweepstakes-rules microcopy that visually resembles the
   side of a cereal box.

### Fictional campaign details

- Cereal pieces: orange corn "craters" and purple oat "moon rocks."
- Marshmallows: pink rockets, green aliens, yellow stars, blue moons, white
  comets.
- Mascot: **Captain Crater**, a grinning moon explorer with an oversized clear
  helmet and spoon-shaped antenna.
- In-box prize wave: five cloth-look **Mission Patches**, one per box.
- Online game: steer the `S.S. Spoon` through a breakfast asteroid field,
  collect marshmallows, avoid soggy milk clouds.
- Score reward: printable `Junior Moon Munch Navigator` certificate. Avoid a
  real external purchase or sweepstakes workflow in the prototype.

### Persona direction

Suggested owner: **`CaptainCrater_MoonMunch`**, officially the mascot, with a
thin layer of overworked brand webmaster underneath.

- Responds to taste, ingredients, prizes, game controls, and missing-patch
  questions.
- Calls the player `Cadet` sparingly and celebrates scores.
- Will admit factual limitations: prizes are random, the cereal contains sugar,
  and customer service cannot identify a prize before opening the box.
- Has intense opinions about cereal becoming soggy and the proper
  cereal-to-milk order.
- Short-response flavor: mission-control phrasing, one exclamation maximum in
  most replies, no repetitive slogans.

## ToonBurst Network - Saturday-morning cartoon block

### Market position

ToonBurst is a free broadcast-network programming block, not a 24-hour cable
channel. It owns Saturday from 8:00 a.m. to noon and runs a smaller weekday
after-school hour. Its site is half TV guide, half fan club, with each show
treated as a different door in a cartoon clubhouse.

Core audience: ages 7-14. The opening hour skews younger and comedic; the middle
hours shift to action/adventure; the final show is the marquee premiere.

Tagline: **SATURDAY STARTS WITH A BURST!**

Secondary lines:

- `Four hours. Zero chores.`
- `New episodes explode every Saturday.`
- `Check local listings - then claim the couch.`

### Visual language

- Source medium: photographed/painted animation cels over flat graphic
  backgrounds, model-sheet poses, registration marks, pencil tests, and
  broadcast promo bursts.
- Palette: near-black `#17142b`, TV cyan `#17c8d8`, hot magenta `#ef326f`,
  golden yellow `#ffd339`, white, with each fictional show assigned one accent.
- Typography: chunky hand-inked logo lettering, condensed schedule type, and
  Verdana for readable descriptions.
- Layout: fixed "control room" or studio stage with a TV-shaped schedule panel,
  circular show badges, a `NOW/NEXT SATURDAY` strip, and character art breaking
  through frames.
- Mix small GIF-like loops with conventional schedule tables. Period sites used
  Flash/Shockwave for compact animated interfaces, but still needed plainly
  readable schedule information.
- Art sheet: one cel-style ensemble key art sheet containing distinct show
  characters but unified by the network's ink/paint pipeline. Include rough
  pencil poses and a cel-with-registration-holes element for the backstage area.

### Site structure

Suggested URLs:

- `web://toonburst.tv/home` - current premiere, show badges, owner thread,
  contest/download teasers.
- `web://toonburst.tv/schedule` - Saturday and weekday grids, episode blurbs,
  local-listing disclaimer, and fan-club downloads.

Homepage modules:

1. Animated-looking network ident and `THIS SATURDAY` premiere card.
2. Four show portals arranged around a CRT control panel.
3. `NOW / NEXT / LATER` schedule strip.
4. Fan-club download: printable membership card or desktop wallpaper.
5. "Inside the Ink" panel showing one pencil pose beside its painted cel.

### Fictional launch schedule

Saturday:

- `8:00` **Mallow & Fang** - timid marshmallow ghost and overconfident monster;
  slapstick comedy, younger-skewing.
- `8:30` **The Odd Jobs Club** - kids whose weekend jobs turn supernatural;
  school-age ensemble comedy.
- `9:00` **Turbo Paladins** - transforming motorcycle knights; action/toyetic.
- `9:30` **Planet Penny** - clever courier crossing handmade alien worlds;
  adventure/comedy.
- `10:00` **Turbo Paladins** repeat or two-part event.
- `10:30` **Captain Catastrophe** - washed-up superhero and competent sidekick.
- `11:00` **Planet Penny** new episode, the marquee premiere.
- `11:30` **ToonBurst Backlot** - shorts, viewer mail, and host interstitials.

Weekdays: `4:00` comedy repeat, `4:30` action repeat. Include `Times may vary.
Check your local ToonBurst station` because real broadcast blocks often varied
by affiliate.

### Persona direction

Suggested owner: **`BurstControl_Kim`**, an enthusiastic junior network
webmaster/programming assistant rather than an in-universe cartoon character.

- Knows schedules, reruns, episode titles, fan-club downloads, and show trivia.
- Can discuss production artifacts such as cels, backgrounds, voice recording,
  and pencil tests without sounding like a textbook.
- Will not promise renewals or reveal unaired plot details; uses phrases like
  `not on the official grid yet`.
- Has a clear favorite (`Planet Penny`) but tries to remain professionally fair.
- Short-response flavor: TV-scheduling vocabulary (`slot`, `premiere`,
  `affiliate`, `bump`), warm and useful, never corporate-mysterious.

## Cross-site worldbuilding opportunities

- Rocketbox advertises **Turbo Paladins** tie-in armor that is "coming soon,"
  while ToonBurst insists no product date has been announced.
- A Moon Munch TV spot sponsors the `9:00` show and offers a ToonBurst-themed
  sixth mission patch.
- ToonBurst's webmaster can complain that the cereal game link only works with
  the newest Shockwave plug-in; Moon Munch support claims it works fine.
- A Rocketbox catalog photo can accidentally show a prototype color, giving the
  product persona something specific to deny or explain later.

These links make the web feel commercially connected without requiring story
canon. They also give the personas a few shared facts that can be added to their
JSON knowledge without merging their voices.
