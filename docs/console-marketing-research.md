# Fictional console campaign research

This expansion uses three competing fictional consoles to capture distinct parts
of the 1999–2001 market without copying a real brand.

## PULSE/NET: online arcade insurgent

PULSE/NET is the $199 underdog: arcade-first, socially connected, loud, and
optimistic about the 56K future. Its white/orange/cyan campaign, included modem,
screen-name language, lobby status, and September-style countdown draw from the
Dreamcast's contemporary value and connectivity pitch.

Useful references:

- [Wired: Dreamcast Coming in September (1999)](https://www.wired.com/1999/04/dreamcast-coming-in-september/)

- [Web Design Museum: Sega in 2002](https://www.webdesignmuseum.org/gallery/sega-2002)

## VANTA²: premium cinematic monolith

VANTA² sells a $299 “second world,” not a toy. Its sparse black/cobalt campaign,
DVD and music positioning, backward compatibility, numbered Flash-like nodes,
and fashion-editorial imagery reflect the PlayStation 2 campaign's effort to
place game hardware alongside adult cinema, nightlife, and culture.

## CUBIT: colorful local pure-play console

CUBIT is the friendly $189 games-only machine. Four controller ports, a carrying
grip, compact discs, chunky controls, colorful cases, polls, stickers, and a
fast conventional page distinguish it from the network pitch and multimedia
pitch. The direction draws from GameCube hardware and Nintendo's playful
web voice at the turn of the century.

## Asset production rule

Campaign art is batched by company, not by content type. Each company gets one
internally consistent contact sheet which is keyed to transparency and cropped
into local assets:

- PULSE/NET: glossy Y2K arcade/product CGI.
- VANTA²: dark fashion-editorial photo collage.
- CUBIT: gouache and cut-paper toy-catalog illustration.

This keeps batch generation efficient without flattening unrelated fictional
brands into the same visual style. Generic interface objects may still share a
sheet when they are not part of a company's campaign identity.
