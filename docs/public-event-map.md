# Orbit: Public Event Map

Use this as lightweight context when writing ordinary public posts, comments,
page updates, and OIM chatter. It contains only what a typical Orbit member
could plausibly observe or repeat. Do **not** treat a rumor as confirmed fact.

## Baseline: before the player arrives

- OrbitOS is an old, mostly forgotten combined desktop/browser community from
  the 1990s. It has a small surviving user base, stale mirrors, old business
  pages, hobby sites, and half-abandoned zones.
- Some businesses still update automatically; many personal sites look years
  out of date. The community is quiet, a little nostalgic, and pleasantly odd.
- A few people trade weird observations: timestamps that do not line up,
  unusual late-night comments, strange old pages, and an occasional page that
  seems more current than it should be. None of this proves anything.

## Phase 1: quiet browsing / the Black File rumor

- A new member is invited to look around Orbit by a friend. Publicly, this is
  just another person joining a tiny old network.
- DarkRaven posts vague, dramatic references to a personal "Black File," odd
  dream-related signals, deleted material, and something he calls the "lower
  room." Most members read this as classic DarkRaven overthinking.
- Mira / Night Signal has receiver logs and late-night signal notes. They are
  eerie but inconclusive; people debate whether they are real interference,
  prank callers, or bad hardware.
- Ordinary life continues: hobby pages update, people argue about games and
  music, and old local business pages get rediscovered.
- Music-minded users occasionally point out that the old Byte Barn commercial
  jingle is surprisingly good. This is a small nostalgia joke at first, not a
  major trend.

## Phase 2: the Orbit revival

- DarkRaven's dramatic dream-invader theory gets shared outside Orbit because
  parts of his evidence look stranger than his conclusion. People show up to
  laugh at it, disprove it, or investigate it for themselves.
- A few days later, Orbit has visibly more traffic. The Newbie Nebula zone and
  fresh personal pages appear; newcomers post about the weird sites, old web
  archaeology, and the community they have stumbled into.
- People notice that a few previously unlisted or forgotten pages are now
  discoverable through search. This is treated as a strange indexing/recovery
  issue, not a confirmed conspiracy.
- Three bigger public rabbit holes become recurring conversation topics:
  **Marrow Five**, **Glass Lake**, and **Quiet County**. Each has scattered,
  contradictory evidence, enthusiastic amateur investigators, and plenty of
  skepticism. Posts should make each sound intriguing, not solved.
- Smaller conspiracies flourish too: changed logos, local cryptids, odd toys,
  eerie commercials, questionable cartoons, and mundane glitches inflated into
  grand theories. Most are playful, low-stakes, and weakly evidenced.
- The Byte Barn jingle begins spreading beyond its original page. Some locals
  remember it; newer users discover it for the first time. Fans make covers and
  remixes, share favorite versions on their own pages, and use **[[barnflip]]**
  as the loose community tag for the fad.
- OrbitNet Services publicly celebrates the traffic bump. Most users take this
  as a normal "the old place is alive again" announcement.

## Phase 3: too much activity

- Orbit gets busier still. New commenters arrive, forgotten usernames appear,
  and old pre-upgrade pages surface through unusual links and comments.
- The atmosphere becomes noisier and less reliable: posts repeat, timing feels
  off, users contradict old claims, and rumors multiply faster than anyone can
  check them. Members disagree whether this is a technical problem, trolls,
  coincidence, or something more deliberate.
- Old technical/government-adjacent documents circulate. They suggest past
  research into attention, social influence, ranking, repetition, and
  distraction. Publicly, the material is incomplete and its significance is
  hotly debated.
- A mysterious professional countdown dominates the directory: it promises a
  music event but initially gives few details. It is clearly connected to the
  sudden popularity of the Byte Barn jingle and its community covers.
- DarkRaven and Night Signal grow more active and more alarmed. They can be
  convinced that something is wrong without claiming they have final proof.

## Phase 4: the Byte Barn event and aftermath

- A major **Byte Barn Forever** compilation CD and one-night music festival are
  announced/released, featuring prominent artists' takes on the old jingle.
  This is a huge, cheerful public event and quickly becomes the dominant topic
  across Orbit.
- At roughly the same time, members circulate serious claims that Orbit has
  been manipulating activity, reviving old accounts, and using rumors to keep
  people engaged. Evidence and arguments are public, but the community is not
  unanimous about every detail.
- The music event overwhelms discussion. Some people are angry about the
  claims; others are tired of conspiracies; many are simply excited that a
  real, friendly community formed around an obscure old site and a silly song.
- Orbit remains online. The immediate public mood is messy but hopeful: keep
  the community, be more honest, enjoy the music, and stop letting every weird
  page become a life-or-death mystery.

## Writing guardrails

- Write in late-1990s / early-2000s internet language. Use fictional,
  in-world names only; do not introduce real brands, platforms, celebrities,
  governments, or locations.
- Public users have observations, rumors, personal reactions, and incomplete
  theories—not omniscient explanations or hidden-system knowledge.
- Vary post length. A quick status-style observation is the minimum; some
  posts can be several short paragraphs of personal context, a project log,
  an anecdote, or a small piece of lore. Do not make every account sound like
  it updates with the same cadence or amount of effort.
- Let characters be wrong, overly confident, distracted, nostalgic, funny,
  or skeptical. The point is a believable community, not a clean exposition
  feed.
- The overall tone is PG-13. Mild curses, crude jokes, teasing, and teen
  language are allowed when they fit a character, but do not make every user
  vulgar and do not use explicit sexual content, slurs, or graphic violence.
- Phase 1 is quiet; Phase 2 is curious and social; Phase 3 is crowded and
  unstable; Phase 4 is a loud, sincere celebration with unresolved arguments
  underneath.

## Authored post cadence

- The Phase 1 count is randomly chosen from one to five, averaging roughly
  three across the network; it is not a fixed count for every user.
- For an active personal or business account, use **one to five** older posts
  visible in Phase 1, then **one or two** new posts in each later phase. Pick
  the count from the owner’s personality, occupation, and how much they use
  the web; it should not be uniform across the network.
- Active hobbyists, organizers, and makers can post more often and write
  longer entries. Casual users may only leave a few short notes.
- Dormant, abandoned, or inaccessible accounts usually have only **one or
  two** genuinely old Phase 1 posts. They have no authored Phase 2 or Phase 4
  posts. If one suddenly updates in Phase 3, the update should be isolated, a
  little too current for the page, and subtly wrong in phrasing or remembered
  detail: corrupted letters, repeated ideas, cheerful nonsense, or a strange
  request to keep browsing. It should create unease, not openly explain
  itself.
- More active characters may notice these revivals as one of the odd things
  happening in Phase 3, usually as a brief observation about an old username
  making a weird post. They should not know the cause.
- Phase 1 post dates are fixed historical dates. Phase 2, 3, and 4 post dates
  are dynamic: preserve each post's authored clock time, but place it on the
  final off-screen day before the player reaches that phase. Phase 2 begins
  after a four-date jump; Phases 3 and 4 each leave one complete off-screen
  calendar day and resume the following morning.
