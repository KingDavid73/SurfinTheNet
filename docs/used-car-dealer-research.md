# Feuding used-car dealership campaign brief

This batch is set in November 1999 and treats two used-car lots on opposite
sides of County Line Road as a small, ugly local media war. Both dealers should
look instantly untrustworthy, but in different ways:

- **King Cal's Auto Kingdom** sells theatrical confidence: crowns, gold paint,
  red carpet, loud weekly-payment numbers, and Calvin Rugg calling every worn
  sedan "royal transportation."
- **Honest Earl's Budget Motors** sells fake humility: handshakes, flags,
  photocopied coupons, the word HONEST everywhere, and Earl Bixby insisting he
  is the consumer's friend while stretching loans far beyond the car's useful
  life.

They occupy neighboring lots, buy competing local television spots, edit their
sites personally, and leave hostile comments on each other's homepages. Neither
one is the trustworthy alternative.

## Period and consumer-protection basis

Buy-here-pay-here financing was an established late-1990s business model in
which the dealership directly financed customers with damaged credit. A
contemporary 1999 trade article described dealers treating it as a potentially
lucrative subprime market.[^wards]

The FTC Used Car Rule requires most dealers to display a Buyers Guide before a
used vehicle is offered for sale. The guide identifies whether the vehicle is
sold as-is or with a warranty, warns that spoken promises are difficult to
enforce, and encourages independent inspection.[^guide] Those facts should
appear in tiny, begrudging legal copy rather than disappearing from the fiction.

The terrible offers should use recognizable deceptive-ad patterns rather than
random nonsense: a seductive weekly payment hiding a long term, a large down
payment, expensive add-ons, an advertised price that only applies under narrow
conditions, and verbal repair promises contradicted by an as-is Buyers
Guide.[^ads]

## Shared visual rules

- Source medium: consumer-grade 35mm color film developed at a one-hour photo
  lab, then cheaply scanned for the web.
- Visible qualities: mild grain, faded color, uneven exposure, halation around
  lot lights, occasional flash glare, dust, and imperfect horizon lines.
- Use tangible late-1980s and early-1990s vehicles with cracked trim, tired
  paint, paper windshield numbers, balloons, pennants, and Buyers Guides.
- Avoid modern dealership architecture, modern cars, pristine cinematic
  lighting, modern HDR, pixel art, or clean vector illustration.
- Generate one 3x2 photographic contact sheet per dealer so their salesmen,
  lots, and photo processing stay internally consistent.
- Keep text and logos out of generated photos. HTML supplies exact names,
  prices, disclaimers, and slogans.

## King Cal's Auto Kingdom

**Owner:** Calvin "King Cal" Rugg, a flamboyant Black local-TV pitchman,
screen name `KingCalCars`.

**Visual identity:** burgundy, tarnished gold, black, and off-white. Crown
clip-art, velvet-rope jokes, starbursts, and a fake heraldic border. Cal wears
an oversized mustard-and-burgundy velvet suit, cheap jewelry, and a plastic
crown. His personal-style Crown Regent is an exhausted homemade show car with
whitewalls, a fake landau roof, plush burgundy velour, a crown hood ornament,
and far too much gold-look trim. The photos look like Cal paid a nephew to
shoot the lot with a point-and-shoot camera. The comedy comes from Cal's
theatrical sales persona and terrible cars, never from racial caricature.

**Voice:** bombastic and wounded. Cal refers to cars as coaches, chariots, and
royal inventory. He calls Earl a liar while giving answers that are technically
true only after reading every line of fine print.

**Inventory examples:**

- 1990 Crown Regent sedan, 142,000 miles: `$1,999 down + $89/week for 156 weeks`
  (`$15,883` before taxes and fees).
- 1988 Family Voyager minivan, 187,000 miles: `$7,995 cash`; air conditioning
  described as "seasonal."
- 1992 Workhorse pickup: `$2,500 down + $109/week for 156 weeks`; mileage
  "exempt" because the odometer only has five digits.
- Financing illustration: `27.9% APR`, `$289 royal preparation fee`, mandatory
  `$395 CrownGuard fabric treatment`, all vehicles sold as-is unless a written
  Buyers Guide says otherwise.

**Feud line:** "Don't cross the road for Earl's handshake. He charges by the
finger."

## Honest Earl's Budget Motors

**Owner:** Earl Bixby, screen name `Honest_Earl`.

**Visual identity:** sun-faded red, white, blue, and cheap green. Photocopier
texture, flag bunting, hand-drawn checkmarks, coupon scissors, and the word
HONEST repeated until it becomes threatening. Earl's photos are overexposed
drugstore prints with date stamps.

**Voice:** falsely intimate and passive-aggressive. Earl calls everyone
"neighbor," complains that Cal is theatrical, and presents his own terrible
terms as kindness.

**Inventory examples:**

- 1989 SunnyBee hatchback, 164,000 miles: `$1,495 down + $79/week for 180 weeks`
  (`$15,715` before taxes and fees).
- 1991 Breeze convertible, 118,000 miles: `$2,000 down + $129/week for 156
  weeks` (`$22,124` before taxes and fees).
- 1987 Family Estate wagon, 176,000 miles: `$6,995 cash`; simulated woodgrain
  included, third-row seat "emotionally available."
- Financing illustration: `26.5% APR`, `$245 friendship filing fee`, mandatory
  `$349 WeatherFriend undercoat`, first payment due before the temporary tag
  cools.

**Feud line:** "King Cal wears a crown because a warranty wouldn't fit on his
head."

## Comment-thread direction

Each homepage begins with a messy public thread:

- The rival dealer challenges an advertised claim.
- The page owner replies defensively and attacks the rival's inventory.
- Two or three dissatisfied customers report concrete mechanical or financing
  problems.
- The owner explains the problem away using the site's known fine print.

The comments should be funny, specific, and incriminating without becoming
cartoon violence. They also give the local model a history to react to when the
player posts.
